**Gravity affects how much time elapses.** In Einstein’s general relativity, space and time form a single structure called *spacetime*. One consequence is **gravitational time dilation**: a clock deeper in a gravitational potential ticks more slowly relative to a clock higher up. Around Earth, this means a clock at sea level runs slightly slower than an identical clock on a mountain. The precise comparison depends on their gravitational potentials and their motion. [damtp.cam.ac.uk](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gr/grhtml/S1.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Near Earth’s surface, the gravitational contribution is approximately:

# ((extra time accumulated by the higher clock) / (elapsed time)) ≈ (g * h) / (c ^ 2)

\[

{\text{elapsed time}}
